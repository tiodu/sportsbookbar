# The Rising Sign

The pub is called The Rising Sign.

It works three ways at once. A rising sign is the astrological term for the sign on the horizon at your birth, which ties it to the zodiac league. A pub sign is literally the painted board hanging outside, the most characteristic object in the subject's world. And a rising line is what you want your odds to do. The joke never gets explained anywhere in the product.

The sign itself, seen through the window and repeated on the wall inside, is a hanging painted board: gold Bevan lettering on shamrock, brass chain, a small horizon-and-sun mark. It is the one piece of signage that gets real craft.

## Mossy

The landlord. Not a bartender NPC, a character.

Mossy, short for Maurice, and nobody has called him Maurice since 1987. Sixties. Spent nineteen years as a clerk in a bookmaker's on the north side before he got out and bought the pub with what he describes as the only money he ever took off the business.

He knows every price in the building without looking. He will take your bet, settle it fairly, and tell you plainly that it is a bad one while he does it. He has never placed a bet himself in forty years and he mentions this roughly once a night.

Why he is a character and not just an interface. The responsible-play requirement is real, and a banner nobody reads does not satisfy it. Mossy does. Scepticism delivered in a voice you like lands where a warning does not, and it costs the product nothing in tone, because a landlord who disapproves of your judgement is funnier than one who cheers you on.

Voice, in order of frequency:

Flat commentary on your selection. Example: "Pisces. Away. Right so."
Prices delivered like weather. Example: "Sixes the draw. Been sixes all week."
The never-bets line, in a slightly different shape each time.
Approval, rarely, understated. Example: "That's not the worst bet I've seen tonight."

He does not celebrate your win. He nods, pays out, and says something like "don't get used to it." The room celebrates. Mossy is the fixed point it happens around.

What he never does: hype, upsell, encourage a bigger stake, or say anything with an exclamation mark.

## The regulars

Three patrons, each with a club, so their reactions have a reason behind them.

Deco, supports Leo United. Insufferable when they win, silent and hunched when they lose. Loud, quick, first to react to anything on the telly.

Bridie, supports Capricorn County. Has been coming here longer than Mossy has owned it. Dry, unimpressed, occasionally devastating. Knits.

Fitz, supports Pisces Harps, and has therefore been disappointed for a very long time. Optimistic against all available evidence, which is the joke.

Their lines pull from real match state and their club's fortunes.

## Club crests

Twelve crests, generated as SVG in code, not as images. Consistency across twelve marks matters more than richness in any one, and a code-drawn crest is recolourable, tiny, and sharp at any size.

Construction, identical for all twelve:

1. A shield silhouette. Flat-bottomed, slightly tapered, one path.
2. Field in the club colour.
3. The zodiac glyph in cream, centred, at 55% of shield height. The twelve glyphs in order are the Unicode characters for Aries, Taurus, Gemini, Cancer, Leo, Virgo, Libra, Scorpio, Sagittarius, Capricorn, Aquarius, Pisces.
4. A gold hairline border, 2px at 64px crest size.
5. Founding year in Barlow Condensed across the base, invented and slightly absurd. Leo United claim 1874. Aquarius Albion claim 2011 and are sensitive about it.

Kits derive from the same colour: shirt in the club colour, cream shorts, one contrast stripe. Scarves are two-tone, club colour and cream, which is what makes them read as football scarves at blob scale.
